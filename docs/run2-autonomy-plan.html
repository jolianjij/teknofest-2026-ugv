TEKNOFEST 2026 · Unmanned Ground Vehicle · Run 2

Run 2 Autonomy Plan

The autonomous run is won by finishing every stage, not by any one clever perception system. The course is fixed, ordered, and known in advance — so the mission runs off a state machine anchored on IMU and lidar signatures, and every camera is demoted to optional confirmation.

The reframe

Where the effort should actually go

Reading the stage signs is worth zero points.

Nothing in the scoring table pays for identifying a stage sign. The only scored detection is the fire point — the laser target, worth 10. The 60 cm discs are course furniture, not a scored task. So sign recognition belongs in the pipeline as a tie-breaker that confirms a state machine which already knows where it is — never as the single trigger that drives it.

That stays true even though the sign detector now works: on 4,300 frames of real printed-sign footage it confirmed all twelve signs in order at 0.88–0.96 confidence. A working detector is a strong third vote and a genuine safety input — it is still not a thing the mission may depend on alone.

  1. Every stage has a strong non-camera signature

    Six of ten stages announce themselves in the BNO055 alone — sustained roll on the side slope, a pitch spike on the vertical obstacle, periodic vertical acceleration on the bumps, sustained pitch on the incline and decline. Two more are unmistakable in lidar: cones and the sliding door. Only the fording entry and the fire zone need position, and both sit on straight runs.

  2. The U-turns are free odometry anchors

    Dead reckoning without encoders drifts. But a corridor end wall fills the forward lidar arc and is impossible to miss. Reset along-track distance at each U-turn and drift can never accumulate past one 20 m lane.

  3. Time is not the binding constraint — completion is

    Roughly 70 m of travel at even 0.5 m/s is ~140 s. Add the two 2-second hill stops, a door wait, and the laser sequence and the worst case still lands near 250 s against a 900 s limit. Bias every judgment call toward reliability; spend speed only on the open straights, where it is free.

Two cues agreeing is the acceptance rule — a sensor signature plus the along-track estimate. The stage sign, if the camera happens to read one, is a third vote and nothing more.

Localisation: corridor-relative, not global

Skip SLAM in a featureless 3 m box

A global map and AMCL buy nothing in a 20 m corridor of near-identical walls, and they fail in exactly the ways that end runs. Estimate three numbers continuously instead — they are what every behaviour actually consumes:

  • Lateral offset from the corridor centreline — both walls are always in view of the RPLidar.
  • Heading error against the corridor axis — lidar wall angle, fused with BNO055 yaw.
  • Along-track distance since the last anchor — integrated, and reset at every U-turn.

Nav2 then earns its place in exactly one place: the cone slalom, where real path planning around discrete obstacles is the task. Everywhere else a reactive corridor controller is simpler, faster to tune, and harder to break.

The three behaviours that hold the points

160 of ~210 stage points live here

Stage 5 · 50 pts · −5 per contact

Cone slalom

The most conventional problem on the course, and the one that most rewards competent Nav2 configuration rather than novel code. Cones stand 75 cm tall on a 40 cm base — comfortably visible to the lidar at any sane mount height.

Cannot be passed on in the autonomous run, so it has no escape hatch: the fallback is to creep along the widest lidar gap rather than stop.

  1. Cap speed on entry; widen the inflation radius so "no contact" has margin.
  2. Local costmap + MPPI only — no global replanning.
  3. Exit on corridor clear ahead for a full lane width.

Stage 6 · 50 pts · timing-critical

Sliding door

Hug the right wall and enter only after the door has cleared to the right — a left exit or an ambiguous read leaves too little margin and risks a strike.

Do not hard-code the door speed. The spec's "20 m/s" is not physically credible for a parkour-scale door and is already flagged for official Q&A. Measure the real period on site during the 5-minute self-trial window and feed that number in.

  1. Stop at standoff, outside the swept volume.
  2. Watch ≥3 full cycles; estimate period, phase and exit side.
  3. Commit only when predicted clear window > transit time × safety factor.
  4. Abort-and-re-wait is a first-class state, not an error path.

Stage 9 · 50 + 10 pts · −10 if anything moves

Laser engagement

This happens stopped, which conveniently dissolves the USB2 bandwidth conflict — the front camera can go down while the aim camera comes up, because nothing is driving.

The −10 penalty for movement during firing means the brake relays must be engaged and stepper pulse generation actually halted, not merely commanded to zero rate. Three attempts exist across runs, so one clean attempt per run is the right ambition.

  1. Stop, brakes on, confirm zero motion in the IMU.
  2. Swap camera; sweep pan/tilt to acquire.
  3. Centre on the concentric-ring target (aim_tracker.py).
  4. Verify stillness → laser on 1 s → off → swap back.

What the front camera does

Three duties, all now built — two of them verified

Duty 1 · validated on real footage

Read the stage signs

Classical CV finds and validates the red ring; a small ONNX CNN classifies the rectified crop; four hits in six frames confirm it. A stage manager then enforces course order, so a spurious sign 8 after sign 2 cannot launch the wrong behaviour.

Measured: 4,300 frames, all 12 signs confirmed in sequence, 0.88–0.96 confidence, and just 1 frame in 4,300 classified unknown.

Duty 2 · stage 5 · verified on synthetic scenes

Judge safe terrain and find cones

A 2D lidar cannot tell firm ground from a soft verge or a ditch — it only reports that nothing is standing there. The ground-plane heuristic marks terrain that looks like the surface already being driven on.

Cones fall out of that for free: an obstacle is a non-drivable blob standing on drivable ground. Each one's base pixel projects under a flat-ground assumption to a real range and bearing, which is what a planner consumes. No cone detector and no colour assumptions — nothing to break under new lighting.

Duty 3 · stage 6 · verified on synthetic sweeps

Read the door's direction

The whole door strategy turns on entering only after the door clears to the right. Direction is exactly what a 10 Hz lidar gives poorly and a camera gives well.

Frame differencing isolates the only large horizontally-moving object in the corridor; a least-squares fit over twelve centroid samples gives signed velocity, so no single noisy frame can flip the answer — the worst possible failure, since direction decides whether the vehicle commits into a moving gate.

Measured: 32/33 and 31/31 correct on right- and left-moving sweeps, zero misreads, nothing reported on a static scene, and a cycle period of 1.83 s against 1.83 s ground truth.

All three run on one camera that cannot share the USB 2.0 hub with the aim camera. Duties 1–3 are all driving-time work and the aim camera is only needed at a standstill, so the conflict is schedulable — but it is still a single point of failure worth removing.

Failure doctrine

One rule, applied everywhere

No single stage may end the run.

Every stage behaviour carries a hard timeout. On expiry: log it, fall back to slow straight-ahead driving, and wait for the next stage cue. Surrendering 5 points on the vertical obstacle costs 5 points; stalling there costs the run — and completing Run 1 is an explicit gate for reward-ranking eligibility, not merely the efficient priority.

An unreadable sign stops the vehicle.

The classifier's reject class used to be discarded silently, which meant a sign the model could not name was indistinguishable from no sign at all — and the vehicle drove past an instruction it knew was there. It is now published as sign 0 and the stage manager halts on it without advancing the stage sequence, because the point is precisely that we still do not know which stage this is.

Two guards keep this from becoming a nuisance. The candidate must already have passed the red-ring geometry checks, and it must clear a geometry-score floor — the classifier's own confidence is meaningless here, since failing it is what "unknown" means. On the real footage this fired on 1 frame in 4,300, and temporal confirmation still requires four hits in six frames before anything stops.

One caveat worth knowing: the classical template fallback has no reject class at all — it always returns its best-matching digit, so it can never report unknown and this fail-safe simply does not exist in that mode. Do not run the template config as the competition configuration.

The same logic governs the pass-on right. It may be one use across the whole final competition rather than one per run — the wording is ambiguous and worth an official question. Cones and the acceleration road cannot be passed on in the autonomous run regardless, so hold the right in reserve for whichever stage physically stops the vehicle. Spending it on a 5-point obstacle that has beached the robot is the ideal outcome; spending it early on a stage you could have driven is not.

Run day

Where the remaining unknowns get resolved

The 5-minute self-trial is a calibration slot

  • Measure the real sliding-door period — the single most valuable number available on site.
  • Confirm speed calibration on the actual surface.
  • Check lighting against the aim camera before it matters.
  • Arm/disarm sanity, stick centring, gimbal range.

Run 1 is also a data-collection pass

  • Manual run is the ranking gate — drive it conservatively and finish.
  • Record front-camera footage throughout: it is the real course imagery every vision model has been starved of.
  • Note actual stage-to-stage distances against the along-track estimate to check drift.
